# Engineering Briefing Board

> Design recipe adapted from the lemo-opuscar style library (MIT, `styles/LICENSE-lemo-opuscar`). References (grammar only): explainers built on a map wall; walls covered in working notes; drafting
conventions (plan above elevation, dimension lines, hatching).

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

1. **A physical board:** enamel, light falling off from a window, a soft diagonal reflection, ghosts of earlier work,
   the frame and the marker tray.
2. **Real things on it:** cut-out photographs of the real objects, a few millimetres off the board, shadowed by one
   window. They are evidence: never a generated image presented as a real object.
3. **Ink that means something:** graphite for names and rules, blue for water and measurement, red only for the
   decision; rejected ideas erased to ghosts.
4. **One wall, read in order:** arrows, projection lines and leaders lead the eye from item to item.

Not a whiteboard doodle (the subjects are photographs), not a chart page (comparisons are dimension lines in real units
beside real objects), not a slide (no headers, centred bullets or page turns).

## 2. Materials and rendering

- **Board:** procedural enamel with wiped ghost strokes, smudges, a window falloff and a faint diagonal reflection; the
  frame shadows a warm grey wall.
- **Ink** (multiply): flat colour at about 90 % with a lighter core and round caps; marker lettering with seeded tilt
  and baseline jitter; erased words as blurred ghosts at about 7 %.
- **Prints:** photographs from open archives or the user, cut out cleanly, one shared grade (a little less saturated,
  a touch warmer, lifted blacks); thin structure (cables, masts, railings) redrawn as clean lines; shadows along one
  light direction, softer for higher objects. Never enlarged past their pixels.
- **Props:** magnetic tags, magnets, a steel ruler, printed cards, a stamp impression; the tray nearest the viewer.

## 3. Colour logic

- Ground: the enamel and its light falloff; the wall around in neutral warm grey.
- Ink: graphite = names, rules, facts; blue = measurement; red = the decision; grey for drafting lines only.
- Red is rare: two or three marks per page at most, never on a red object. Full colour belongs to the photographs.

## 4. Type

- Marker lettering in a single-weight marker face that covers the language's letters; the verdict 2–3× the size of the notes.
- Printed labels on tags and cards in a sturdy sans; sources in a monospace; a tag holds one to three words.

## 5. Pitfalls

- Photos that don't belong together: one grade, one light direction, one viewpoint family.
- Lettering inside photos (signs, logos): paint it out or choose another photo.
- A sparse wall or one crowded corner: size the hierarchy for the whole page, a quiet secondary layer, no empty middle.

## 6. Variation space

- Structures: one measurement that grows until it breaks a limit; a before and after with the first plan erased to a
  ghost; a checklist stamped item by item.
- Formats: a strategy post, a process one-pager, a workshop poster, a report spread.
