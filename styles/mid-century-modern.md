# Mid-century Modern

> graphic base's own design recipe. References (grammar only): 1950s–60s American and Scandinavian graphic design (Paul Rand, Alvin Lustig, airline and
corporate posters): playful geometry, cut-paper shapes, warm flat colour.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not
- **Simple geometric shapes with wit:** circles, boomerangs, kidney shapes, cut-paper figures; a visual pun.
- **Warm, slightly faded flat colour** with paper texture and off-register overlaps.
- Hand-drawn or quirky display type beside a clean sans.

Not the mid-century cartoon (no characters with faces as the main device), not Bauhaus (warmer, freer, more humour).

## 2. Materials and rendering
- Flat shapes with slightly irregular edges, overprinting where they overlap; a light paper grain.

## 3. Colour logic
- Mustard, teal, tomato, olive, cream and black; three or four per piece.

## 4. Type
- A quirky or hand-drawn display face for one word, a clean geometric or grotesque sans for the rest.

## 5. Pitfalls
- Atomic-starburst overload; too many colours; retro filters instead of real flat shapes.

## 6. Variation space
- Formats: cafes and food, friendly brands, family events, book covers, cultural programmes.
