# Editorial Magazine

> The authority of a well-made magazine spread, on one page or across a report: a confident headline, a photograph
> cropped with intent, columns, a pull quote, rules and folios. References (grammar only): Fabien Baron's Harper's
> Bazaar, Willy Fleckhaus's twen, the long-form features of contemporary print magazines.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

- **One big decision per page:** a headline set very large, or a photograph that takes most of the page, never both
  at the same weight.
- **Columns and rules:** text in 2–3 columns on a visible grid; hairline rules separate sections; a folio and running
  head mark the page as part of a publication.
- **The pull quote** is the second read: a short line from the text, set big in the display face, with air around it.
- Not a blog layout with cards; not a slide; not centred everything.

## 2. Materials and rendering

- A photograph from the user or open archives, cropped tight (a face's eyes on the upper third, an object filling its
  box edge to edge); duotone or black and white when colour photographs fight the palette.
- Hairline rules (0.25–0.5 u on screen, 0.25 pt in print), drop caps of 3 lines, small caps for kicker lines.
- Print: uncoated feel by a warm white ground; coated feel by a pure white ground and rich photography.

## 3. Colour logic

- Mostly ink on paper; one accent for kickers, rules or the drop cap; the photograph carries the colour.
- A page tinted in a single flat colour behind type is the magazine's way to change voice between sections.

## 4. Type

- A display serif with real contrast (high-contrast didone, a sharp transitional or a modern wedge serif) for
  headlines and quotes; a quiet text serif or grotesque for body at 9–10.5 pt in print, 30–34 px on a 1080 post.
- Headlines tight (−1 to −3 % tracking, leading 0.9–1.0); kickers in small caps, tracked +8 %.
- Hierarchy: kicker → headline → standfirst → body → caption, each a clear step.

## 5. Pitfalls

- Too many sizes; a pull quote that repeats the headline; body text running full width; a photograph shrunk to fit
  instead of cropped to say something; captions forgotten.

## 6. Variation space

- Swiss-leaning (grotesque, asymmetric, big white space) · fashion (didone, huge photo, tiny text) · literary
  (serif, centred title page, ornaments) · news (dense columns, rules everywhere, a big number).
