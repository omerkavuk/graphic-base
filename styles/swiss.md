# Swiss International Style

> Design recipe adapted from the lemo-opuscar style library (MIT, `styles/LICENSE-lemo-opuscar`). References (grammar only): 1950s–70s Zurich and Basel posters (Müller-Brockmann, Hofmann, Gerstner, Crouwel):
the grid, flush-left type, scale contrast, "designing programmes"; contemporary identity systems built on the same
grid. Never a specific historical poster's composition, never Helvetica or Akzidenz files, never a real institution's
name on a poster that is not theirs.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

- **Pure graphic design on a grid:** flat paper, black type and bars, a very light grey and **one** saturated signal
  colour.
- **Every element sits on the grid;** nothing floats.
- **Rigour with one exception:** the whole page obeys a system, so the one element that doesn't becomes the subject.
- **Extreme scale contrast:** a giant numeral beside tiny technical notes.

Not a slide (no gradients, glow or device renders), not a chart page (bars are graphic form unless the grid gives them
meaning), not a Bauhaus pastiche of primary shapes.

## 2. Materials and rendering

- **Page grid:** 12 columns, side margins ~5 % of the width, gutters ~1.25 %, a baseline grid; a wide page can act as a
  spread: a left half for the programme (numeral, rule, notes) and a right half for the artifact.
- **Artifact grid** derived from the content: what the columns and rows mean is a design decision; fewer, wider modules
  give elements mass (too many thin columns read as a chart).
- **Surfaces:** page, paper, ink, signal colour, grid lines in two or three light greys; no gradients, shadows,
  particles or grain.
- **Bars and blocks** as flat rectangles; cells of one unit merged into one bar.
- **A secondary voice** in light grey under the black: depth without a second colour.

## 3. Colour logic

- Paper, ink, greys and one signal colour; only one thing carries it, and it is the only thing allowed to be inexact.
- Black type may overprint the signal colour.
- Signal red, orange, ultramarine or green, chosen for the topic; never a second.
- Examples: `#EAE9E5` page, `#FFFFFF` paper, `#111111` ink, signal `#E30613`; `#F2F0EA` / `#141414` / `#FF5A00`;
  `#E9ECEF` / `#0B0B0B` / `#1F3FFF`.

## 4. Type

- **One family for the whole piece:** an open neo-grotesque (Archivo, Schibsted Grotesk, Public Sans, Instrument Sans;
  check the AI-overused list in `craft.md` §6), several weights; tracking about −2 % at large sizes; headlines may be
  lowercase.
- **Flush left, ragged right,** everything on a grid line, big white space as part of the layout; giant numerals
  optically flush left (minus the glyph's left bearing).
- **Caption rows:** small medium-weight notes with a 1 px rule above; technical notes in a corner (module sizes,
  counts, a date).

## 5. Pitfalls

- Too many thin columns look like a chart: fewer, wider modules.
- Merely tilting is not a composition: one strong diagonal with a long-short rhythm, a larger signal element, a bleed,
  type over colour.
- Rotated text plus labels at once reads as noise: one of them.

## 6. Variation space

- Structures: a timetable (24 rows of hours); an alphabet specimen; a comparison across a split spread until one element
  crosses the gutter.
- Formats: a corporate report, a conference poster, an exhibition banner, a typographic post series.
