# Technical Manual

> graphic base's own design recipe. References (grammar only): the online reference manual Making Software (Dan Hollick): exploded technical drawings in one blue, a pixel display face and a bookish serif.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not
- **The drawing explains:** one object taken apart into layers (exploded view), drawn in thin single-colour line with flat tints, each part labelled with a leader line.
- **Manual furniture:** figure numbers set vertically in the margin (FIG. 001), a dotted rule under the title, small caption labels in monospace capitals.
- **Reading calm:** a narrow column of serif text beside big drawings; plenty of white.

Not a blueprint (no dark blue ground, no grid paper), not a cartoon infographic (no colour fills, no characters).

## 2. Materials and rendering
- Line work 1 to 1.5 px at 1080 px, one weight; tints at 10 to 30 % of the line colour for planes.
- Isometric or axonometric projection; layers spaced apart on a shared vertical axis with thin dashed guides.
- A dotted or dashed rule as the only ornament.

## 3. Colour logic
- Near-white ground (#f7f8fa or warm #fafaf7), one strong blue for all line work and the headline (#2b4dff, #1f3fd6), black or dark grey for text.
- A second colour only for one highlighted part, if at all.

## 4. Type
- Headline in a pixel or monospace display face, letter-spaced capitals (Silkscreen, Space Mono, IBM Plex Mono, VT323 for a bolder pixel).
- Text in a small, bookish serif with a drop cap (Newsreader, Source Serif 4, Libre Caslon); labels in monospace capitals at the legal size or above.

## 5. Pitfalls
- Drawings too detailed to read at post size: three to six parts, big.
- Blue text on blue tints: keep labels on white.

## 6. Variation space
- Formats: explainers, how-it-works posts, product and science one-pagers, report spreads, carousels that take one thing apart per slide.
- Take the grammar (palette logic, type, spacing, structure), never the reference's layout.
