# Bauhaus

> The school's graphic grammar (1919–1933). References (grammar only): Herbert Bayer, Joost Schmidt, László
> Moholy-Nagy's typophoto, the 1923 exhibition poster.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

- **Circle, square, triangle** as the building blocks; red, yellow, blue with black and white.
- **Asymmetric balance:** heavy shapes against open space, type on horizontal and vertical axes, bars and rules as
  structure, sometimes one strong diagonal.
- **Typophoto:** a black and white photograph cut and combined with type and shapes.
- Not "geometric shapes as decoration": every shape holds or points at something.

## 2. Materials and rendering

- Flat SVG shapes with exact geometry; a printed-paper texture only if the card names it.
- Photographs in black and white, high contrast, cut out on a hard edge.

## 3. Colour logic

- Primaries plus black and a paper white (warm off-white for print character). One primary dominates; the other two
  are small.

## 4. Type

- Geometric sans serif (a Futura-like or Bayer-like universal face; check the language's letters), lower case welcome, bold
  weights for headlines, bars and dots as punctuation of the layout.
- Type rotated 90° along an edge is native.

## 5. Pitfalls

- Equal amounts of the three primaries (it becomes a toy); centring everything; using pastel versions of the primaries.

## 6. Variation space

- Strict (black, one red, grid) · playful (primaries, big circle, rotated type) · typophoto (photo cut-out with bars) ·
  contemporary (one primary on a deep neutral, generous space).
