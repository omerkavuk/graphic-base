# Paper Explainer Collage

> Design recipe adapted from the lemo-opuscar style library (MIT, `styles/LICENSE-lemo-opuscar`). References (grammar only): editorial-collage explainers of the 2010s–20s; newspaper clipping files; a
researcher's desk with highlighters. Recipe first written for cinecode, after charlie947/motion-graphics-skills (MIT).

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

- **A question, answered on paper:** the piece asks one "why" a stranger half understands and lands on a takeaway of
  five words or fewer; every element proves something.
- **Everything is a printed object:** photo cut-outs with a white border, document scans, charts on cream stock, sticky
  labels. Depth from paper layers and soft shadows, never glow.
- **The facts are real:** every name, date and number from an approved source (`research/facts.md`); a similarity is
  said as "looks like", never "is".

Not a halftone dossier (no comic stamps or gags; a calm investigation), not an archive light table (paper lies on a
desk), not Swiss (no clean grid on white; warm, layered, a little messy).

## 2. Materials and rendering

- **Desk and paper:** a warm cream ground with low mottling and fine grain; every card a flat fill with a torn edge (a
  seeded jagged path, 2–5 px teeth at 1080 px) and a soft drop shadow (6–10 px offset, 12–18 px blur, 25–35 % ink).
- **Cut-outs:** photos and objects with an 8–14 px white border and the same shadow; real images only with a licence
  row, otherwise flat silhouettes drawn in code.
- **Print texture:** a halftone screen on photos and big fills (dots 6–10 px, one angle) and paper grain over all.
- **Marker:** a felt-tip stroke, slightly wobbly, 6–10 px, round caps, for circles, underlines and arrows.
- **Highlight:** a yellow bar behind a word, uneven at both ends.
- **Counters and charts** drawn from the real numbers on cream cards.

## 3. Colour logic

- Cream ground, one navy ink for type and lines, one signal red for the marker and the key word, one yellow highlighter;
  photos toned toward the paper.
- Default (label it as one): red `#B8312A`, cream `#F1E6D0`, paper `#FBF7EE`, navy `#14213D`, yellow `#F2C230`; a brand
  brings its own four colours into the same roles. No gradients on type, no glow.

## 4. Type

- **Statement type:** a bold condensed sans in caps on a torn paper strip, three short lines at most.
- **Body and labels:** a sturdy text face in navy; labels on small white tags with a pin or tape.
- Sources in the piece's legal line or the post text, never as tags on the cards.

## 5. Pitfalls

- One small element on an empty card reads as unfinished: cards, images or objects in layers.
- Lopsided grids look like mistakes: equal card sizes and gaps.
- Logos from the real files, sized to their card.
- An unsourced number breaks the genre: no source, no number.

## 6. Variation space

- Palette: the default, a brand's four colours, or a "night desk" with a navy ground and cream type.
- Texture: clean (few photos, more type) to dense (clippings three deep).
- Formats: a carousel (one fact per slide), a 4:5 explainer post, a report spread, a 9:16 story with stacked strips.
