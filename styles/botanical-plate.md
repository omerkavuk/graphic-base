# Botanical Plate

> A natural-history plate: a specimen shown with care on a quiet page, labelled like a museum would. References
> (grammar only): 18th–19th century botanical and zoological plates in the Biodiversity Heritage Library, museum
> specimen cards.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

- **The specimen is the hero:** a plant, shell, insect, bird or object, centred or in a numbered arrangement, on a
  warm white ground with generous margins.
- **Labels:** a Latin name and the common name in small caps or italic, figure numbers, a fine rule frame, perhaps a plate
  number in a corner.
- Not watercolour clip-art; not a busy floral pattern.

## 2. Materials and rendering

- Real plates from open archives: `asset_search.py search --q "<species> botanical illustration" --type illustration`
  (Openverse brings the Biodiversity Heritage Library; also `aic`, `cleveland`, `smithsonian`). Public domain plates are
  the first choice; cut out the specimen when the ground fights the palette.
- Or a specimen drawn in code as fine line work (SVG paths, hatching in thin strokes).
- Paper texture: a light warm sheet, multiplied.

## 3. Colour logic

- Paper white or ivory, ink in a warm dark brown or black, and the specimen's own colours; an accent from the
  specimen (a berry red, a leaf green) for the title.

## 4. Type

- A classical serif with a good italic (old-style or transitional) for names; small caps for labels; a modest size
  hierarchy. Numerals in old-style figures.

## 5. Pitfalls

- A specimen too small in a big empty page; Latin names spelled wrong; ornamental frames heavier than the specimen.

## 6. Variation space

- Single specimen centred · a grid of numbered figures · a specimen cut out on a flat colour field (modern) · an
  index-card label as the layout.
