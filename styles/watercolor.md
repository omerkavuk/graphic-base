# Watercolor Brush

> Design recipe adapted from the lemo-opuscar style library (MIT, `styles/LICENSE-lemo-opuscar`). References (grammar only): naturalist field journals and botanical watercolours (a specimen and its note);
long horizontal handscroll panoramas; the textbook "transect" diagram. Never a specific artwork or layout.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

- **One sheet of warm, fibrous paper** under everything.
- **Everything is individual brush strokes:** a variable-width ribbon at partial opacity with a few thin bristle tracks
  that break into dashes (flying white); strokes overlap and darken where they cross, like transparent pigment. No
  vector outlines: a darker edge stroke along a form is the only "line".
- **Structure before detail:** trunk, branches, leaves; outline wash, shadow, accent.
- **An annotation layer** in a hand the painter would write with: labels with pen leaders, a small measured value, a
  quiet title.
- Calm, curious, precise; numbers are real; the palette can carry data.

Not ink wash (colour, not ink tones), not impasto, not a crayon picture book, not vector art with a watercolour texture.

## 2. Materials and rendering

- **Paper:** a warm base, three octaves of smooth noise (±3.5 %), a few thousand short curved fibres, fine grain, a light
  vignette.
- **Stroke:** a ribbon whose width follows a profile (brush, leaf, even, tip, trunk) with ~±22 % roughness, a thin wash
  body (~55–60 % with bristles) and 3–9 bristle tracks at 30–85 % with random dashes and early ends (fixed seed).
- **Wash:** the same shape painted in three passes with jittered edges at a third of the opacity each, so the edges
  darken where the passes disagree (a pooled edge).
- **Depth by layers:** farther = smaller, lighter, less saturated; paper-coloured mist bands between layers.
- **Hand notes** with a pen leader growing to the specimen, over a paper-coloured halo.

## 3. Colour logic

- **Paper is the white:** never paint white; light is unpainted paper.
- **Transparent layering:** darker = more passes, not a darker pigment on top; 3–5 hues per piece plus ink.
- **Ink** (a warm near-black and a lighter brown) for lines, text, edges and stems only.
- **Colour may carry data or mood:** ramps quantised into 4–6 bands read as paint; continuous ramps read as software.
- **One accent** (a vermilion, a rain blue) for one element that recurs.
- Examples: arid to wet: red ochre, sage, straw, blue-grey, deep green; a harbour at dawn: `#f3e2c8` sky, `#9fb4c4` water,
  `#5f7486` hulls, accent `#c8442a`; a garden: `#e8e0c0` soil, `#8ea860` leaves, `#4e6e3c` shade, accent `#d9632e`.

## 4. Type

- An elegant serif for titles, a handwriting face for notes, a small wide-tracked mono for measured labels.
- Text sits on the paper, never in a box: a serif italic, two balanced lines at most, ink at ~78 % over a paper halo.

## 5. Pitfalls

- A wash ending at a hard edge shows a seam: fade it to paper.
- Painting every corner: leave paper; the white is the light.
- Bright saturated washes look digital: fewer passes, quieter hues, one accent.

## 6. Variation space

- Structures: a journal page per month; one specimen dissected into labelled parts; a recipe or process painted top to
  bottom on one tall sheet.
- Formats: a floral wedding invitation, a spring event poster, a garden or wellness brand post, a menu.
