# Cartographic

> The map as the layout. References (grammar only): classic topographic sheets, Swiss national maps, transit maps,
> antique sea charts.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

- A real geography drawn from data: coastlines, borders, rivers, roads; contour lines for relief; one route, region or
  set of points that carries the message.
- The furniture of maps: a legend, a scale bar, a north arrow or compass rose, a grid with coordinates, numbered
  points with a key.
- Not a screenshot of a map service (never placed: `sources.md` §3); not decorative fake maps.

## 2. Materials and rendering

- Shapes from Natural Earth (public domain) or OpenStreetMap (ODbL: the credit line on the design), drawn as SVG paths.
- Relief: contour lines as fine strokes; hatching for an antique chart; flat tints for a modern one.
- Labels along features (a river's name follows its line), place names in small caps.

## 3. Colour logic

- Land and water in close, quiet tones (paper and a pale blue, or two greys); one strong colour for the route or the
  places that matter.

## 4. Type

- A humanist or grotesque sans for modern maps; a classical serif with italic for water names on antique charts;
  sizes tiered by importance of place.

## 5. Pitfalls

- Too much detail at a small size; labels colliding; a map that does not say where the viewer should look; wrong
  projection stretching the region.

## 6. Variation space

- Locator (a small region, one point, the address) · route (a line from A to B with stops) · transit diagram
  (45° lines, stations) · antique chart (compass rose, hatching, serif labels) · data map (regions tinted by a value).
